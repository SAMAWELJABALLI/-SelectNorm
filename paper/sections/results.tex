\subsection{Selective normalisation against strong baselines (RQ1, RQ2)}
\label{sec:main-results}
Always normalising (B1) improves over the original input on the Tunisian and Algerian sets for every encoder, by 2.3--18.6 points (Table~\ref{tab:main}); on MYC it gains 8.8 points with XLM-R and loses 7.9 with LaBSE. Learned routing adds no consistent improvement. Against B4 no difference is significant and every 95\% interval lies inside $[-0.63,+0.58]$, within the 1-point margin in all 16 cells (a post hoc equivalence reading); SelectNorm-adapt and -src differ by $-0.25$ to $+0.54$ (three significant). Against B1 and B3, SelectNorm-adapt is relevantly better in one cell (LaBSE, MYC: $+5.5$, $+5.0$), where normalising harms and thresholding does not stop it, and relevantly worse in one (XLM-R, MYC: $-3.8$), where it keeps the original view for about 40\% of inputs although normalising is right; in the LaBSE MYC cell it is relevantly worse than B0, the better view there ($-2.5$). Elsewhere only 4 (B1) and 6 (B3) of 16 intervals lie within $\pm1$, and the other non-significant ones span $-1.3$ to $+2.4$, so small gains or losses cannot be excluded.

B0 costs 1.00 and B1 0.97--1.04 passes (the normaliser adds about 0.4\%). At $\lambda=0$ SelectNorm-adapt costs 1.69--1.88 passes for mE5 and 1.43--1.88 across encoders, against 1.89--2.05 for the ensemble; raising $\lambda$ lowers the cost (mE5: 1.14--1.45 at $\lambda=0.2$) while macro-F1 falls towards B0 (Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}). In Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} every router operating point lies between the single-view references (B0, B1) and R in estimated cost: routing saves compute relative to running both views, never relative to one view, and is never relevantly more accurate than the better single view (at most $+0.6$, not significant). At matched $\lambda$, SelectNorm and B4 differ by $-0.7$ to $+1.2$ points and by at most 0.09 passes (descriptive; only $\lambda=0$ is tested). Timing the deployed pipeline (200 sentences per set; live decisions reproduce all 184{,}400 archived ones) agrees: B0 22--31\,ms; B1 0.91--1.05, SelectNorm-adapt 1.31--1.97, R 1.91--2.07 passes. Thresholding with each input's own cost $\lambda c_1(x)$ (exploratory) changes macro-F1 by $-2.0$ to $+0.6$ and cost by 0 to $-0.11$ passes, moving along the same trade-off curve (no worse on both in 29 of 80 cells, trading accuracy for cost in 48).

\paragraph{(M1) Disagreement is moderately predictable.} 21--39\% of inputs change label under normalisation; label-free $\hat\pi$ ranks them with AUC 0.62--0.74, all intervals within $[0.57,0.77]$ (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}, Fig.~\ref{fig:diagmap}). Target re-estimation helps most on MYC (mE5: 0.67 to 0.74), but the rule at $\lambda=0$ depends on $\hat\rho$ alone.

\paragraph{(M2) Direction is weakly predictable from $z$ with linear estimators, also with target labels.} Source $\hat\rho$ reaches AUC 0.54--0.71 in 14 of 16 cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:diagmap}) (upper bounds $\le0.76$); for XLM-R the interval includes 0.5 on Algerian text and lies below it on Moroccan text ($[0.41,0.48]$). The target-trained cross-validated reference, which uses target labels unavailable to the zero-shot router, reaches 0.53--0.68 (upper bounds $\le0.73$; five intervals include 0.5). What changes across domains is the \emph{level} of $\rho$: the win rate ranges from 33\% to 77\%, and source $\hat\rho$ is miscalibrated out of domain (gaps up to $+20$ points; intervals exclude zero in 11 of 12 cells).

\paragraph{(M3, M4) Benefit and headroom.} The rule $\hat\rho>\nicefrac12$ gains $-3.3$ to $+1.0$ points over the best view with source estimates (one interval above zero, three below) and $-0.7$ to $+2.8$ with the target-trained reference (upper bounds $\le+3.9$): most of the gap to the oracle remains when the router is fitted on target labels. The oracle gains 7--17 points over the best view, but two classifiers differing only in a bootstrap resample of their training data show 3--15 points of such headroom; this reference depends on how the classifiers are made (2--6 with 80\% subsamples, 5--22 with weaker regularisation) and is not a decomposition.

\paragraph{Linguistic conditions.} On Tunisian and Algerian sets, normalising helps less on inputs with many French or English tokens or low lexicon coverage (except XLM-R on TUNIZI for French/English); negation and length show no consistent pattern; on MYC every condition changes sign with the encoder.

\subsection{Few target labels (RQ6), ablations and classifier regime}
\label{sec:fewlabel}
With $k$ labelled target sentences, re-estimating the shift of $\hat\rho$ changes macro-F1 relative to zero-shot SelectNorm-adapt by $-1.4$ to $+1.5$ points across encoders, regimes, sets and $k$ (mean $-0.01$); although targeted labels are $1/P(D{=}1)$ times more informative about the shift (Proposition~\ref{prop:efficiency}), the resulting macro-F1 is within $-0.1$ to $+0.2$ points of random labels at every $k$ (averaged, descriptive). In the Arabic-script regime SelectNorm beats a set-level choice between B0 and B1 by 2.6--2.9 points on average for $k\le16$ and by 0.3 at $k=128$, consistent with M2.

\label{sec:normaliser}
\paragraph{Ablations (mE5, $\lambda=0$).} The safeguards set the quality of the views the router chooses between. Without them, always normalising loses 4.2--6.2 points ($T_{\rm lex}$) and 5.2--10.6 ($T_{\rm rule}$) on every set, and routing over $T_{\rm lex}$ stays 0.6--4.6 points below routing over $\Tcons$. Keeping out-of-lexicon tokens in Latin script ($T_{\rm cov}$) reduces disagreements by about a third; it is worse than $\Tcons$ on the Tunisian sets and slightly better on $S_5$ and $S_6$. Routing components matter less. Removing $\hat\pi$ changes macro-F1 by at most 0.1 point, as Sect.~\ref{sec:formulation} implies for $\lambda=0$, dropping its target re-estimation by at most 0.2, removing one feature group by $-0.5$ to $+0.7$, and gradient boosting by $-0.6$ to $+0.7$. Two label-free simplifications ($\hat\pi$ alone; confidence features alone) stay within 0.2 points of always normalising on every set and 0.6--0.7 below the full router on $S_6$. \paragraph{Human transliterations (RQ4) and \textsc{+syn}.} On NArabizi, B1 agrees with $f$'s prediction on the human transliteration for 73--77\% of inputs (B0: 61--67\%; SelectNorm-adapt: 73--76\%), on which $f$ reaches 65--77 macro-F1. In \textsc{+syn} the original view improves and the win rate on disagreements nears 50\%; SelectNorm-adapt is relevantly better than the better view once with Holm correction (MARBERTv2, $S_4$, $+1.1$), twice more unadjusted (mE5, $S_3$ $+2.1$, $S_4$ $+1.4$), and relevantly worse in two MYC cells under both (LaBSE $-2.4$, MARBERTv2 $-1.8$); it is never significantly better than B4 (worse once, $-0.4$).
