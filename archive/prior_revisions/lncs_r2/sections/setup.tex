\paragraph{Data and classifiers.}
$f$ is trained on Arabic-script TSAC~\cite{medhaffar2017tsac}; the human Latin-script TSAC training comments, never seen by $f$, form the policy pool (Table~\ref{tab:data}). Test sets cover the same corpus ($S_3$), another Tunisian corpus and platform ($S_4$, TUNIZI~\cite{fourati2021tunizi}), Algerian ($S_5$, NArabizi~\cite{seddah2020narabizi,touileb2021interplay}) and Moroccan Arabizi ($S_6$, MYC~\cite{jbel2023myc}). Mixed-script sentences (minority alphabet $>20\%$) are excluded. $f_\theta$ is a logistic regression on mean-pooled frozen representations of XLM-R base~\cite{conneau2020xlmr}, mE5 base~\cite{wang2024me5}, LaBSE~\cite{feng2022labse} or MARBERTv2~\cite{abdulmageed2021marbert} at pinned revisions, with regularisation and a temperature~\cite{guo2017calibration} fitted on the 10\% Arabic-script development split. No Latin-script sentence trains $f$. In an exploratory regime (\textsc{+syn}), logged as a deviation before it was run, $f$ also sees a synthetic Latin rendering of each training sentence.

\paragraph{Systems.}
B0 keeps $x$; B1 always normalises with $\Tcons$; B2 routes at random at SelectNorm's selection rate (20 draws); B3 thresholds the confidence of $p_0$; B4 estimates $B(z)=P(\text{help}\mid z)-P(\text{harm}\mid z)$ directly, a post-hoc deferral rule~\cite{jitkrittum2023cascade}; B4b predicts each view's error~\cite{kim2020learningloss}; B6 is the oracle; R averages both views' probabilities. SelectNorm-src uses source estimates; SelectNorm-adapt, the primary system, re-estimates $\hat\pi$ on unlabelled target text with two-fold cross-fitting by source sentence. All thresholds are tuned per $\lambda$ on the policy development split (variant: Bayes threshold $\lambda\bar c$), never on a test set.

\paragraph{Few-label setting.}
Each test set is split into two folds by source sentence; to predict one fold, $k\in\{8,\ldots,128\}$ labels are drawn from the other, at random or among its disagreements, and re-estimate $b$ (SelectNorm), re-fit B4, re-tune B3, or choose B0 or B1 for the fold (set-level choice). A replay of all 8{,}000 draws found no sentence whose own label informed its prediction. On $S_5$ at $k{=}128$ only 97--116 disagreements exist per fold, so 375 targeted draws are smaller than $k$. Each $k$ uses 5 draws $\times$ 5 seeds and the $\lambda=0$ threshold.

\paragraph{Mechanisms, statistics and costs.}
We measure (M1) the AUC of $\hat\pi$ for disagreements; (M2) the AUC of $\hat\rho$ for the winning view among disagreements, its calibration gap, and a \emph{target-trained cross-validated reference} (same features and estimator family, 5-fold cross-validation on target labels); (M3) the gain over the best view of the rule $\hat\rho>\nicefrac12$; (M4) the oracle headroom, against the headroom between two classifiers trained on bootstrap resamples and applied to the same input. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 1{,}000 seed-averaged bootstrap resamples of test sentences, without refitting. Macro-F1 is primary; results average five seeds (policy split and random draws). Differences are tested with a paired bootstrap over source sentences~\cite{koehn2004bootstrap,dror2018hitchhiker} (10{,}000 resamples) with Holm correction over the 20 main comparisons per encoder. A difference is \emph{relevant} if it is at least one point and significant after correction; the protocol's wording requires only the unadjusted interval to exclude zero, which coincides in the primary regime (\textsc{+syn}: both reported). Non-significance is not read as equivalence. Costs are relative to one pass of $f$ on $x$: $c_T$ is measured; $c_0,c_1$ are \emph{estimated} by a per-encoder model $a+b\cdot\text{tokens}$ fitted to batch-1 CPU latencies and checked by end-to-end timing.
